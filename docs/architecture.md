# module-kit — architecture

> What the component does, what it does not do, and what is still to decide about it. This is the detail of its card, the one in `spec/docs/architecture.md` whose heading names `module-kit`. The card stays the summary: if the detail changes what the card says, the card is changed in `spec` the same way as the questions for its registers, by a row «В реестр spec» of [run/QUESTIONS.md](../run/QUESTIONS.md).
>
> Why it was decided so and not otherwise is in [decisions.md](decisions.md); the work plan is in [RUN.md](../RUN.md). The concepts are those of the glossary of `spec/docs/concepts.md`. The documents of `spec` are under `spec/docs/`, and a record of its sources or measurements is cited with the suffix "spec". A fact no run of ours checks — a provider's terms, a price, a law, a page with no version — becomes a record of [sources.md](sources.md), with its address, the date read and a quote; a library's documentation or code at a version or commit is a link beside the claim, a test or a measurement its evidence.

## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does** — by the card:
- holds the semantics of the module format — one library for all consumers. The verdict and the check of keys at publication depend on one code of the check kinds. Through updates, the determinism of the verdict is held by the format's test vectors — `format/vectors/` of `spec`: CI of `spec` passes them with its second implementation, and CI of `module-kit` passes them all;
- reads an author's module — YAML 1.2.2 with the format's constraints — and checks it against JSON Schema 2020-12 and the format rules. A refusal names the rule's ID. What is checked, the card lists:
  - the schema, the skill graph without cycles, the references, the anchor skills of the shared taxonomy and the manifest's dependencies;
  - the tasks, the slot checks, the trap and technique catalogs, the lessons;
  - the templates with all their instances and their solvers;
  - the author's texts that the learner sees before solving — by the leak check.

  Of a picture with transparent pixels the CLI warns but does not refuse. Of a number in a text of the whole module — a rung template, a trap advice, a technique, a solving step — it also warns but does not refuse ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). The check also holds the page of a workbook — the layout by `page.yaml` and the rules `task.workbook-page` and `lesson.workbook-page` —, a worked example on a workbook with `lesson.example-fills`, the kind `time`, the flag `notation: word` of a choice written by hand, the picture paths of a template with placeholders, the leading question and the description of a step in the leak check, the numbered list of a lesson and the rule `yaml.printable` ([format text](../spec/format/README.md));
- reads the module format versions in the window. While the format is v0, the window holds only the current version, the one the format text of the submodule names, and a module of an earlier version is rejected. A module is checked, built and executed by the version that its manifest names, and a module outside the window is rejected with a reason;
- builds the module package and the module index:
  - the package — the canonical view with the tier of each skill and the numbers of each instance of a template, the device part and the inventory;
  - the index — from the previous release's index, with an entry of each published version by its package digest and with the marks of withdrawal.

  The package is a signed, content-addressed version of a module, and the index is a signed cumulative list of the published versions. At a release of `modules` they are built by the CLI of `module-kit`;
- compares a new version of a module with each published one, withdrawn ones too, by the format's rules of version comparison, and checks retired skills: a withdrawal does not free the skills' IDs ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). A package of a previous format version it reads by the places that the format keeps in all versions, and across a format transition it checks the skills and the IDs the same way ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). The version part the comparison finds itself, and the CLI has a command of its own for it, `diff` (see version comparison below);
- fills task templates:
  - goes through the combinations of parameters — no more than 10,000 per template — and builds the instances;
  - the key of each instance is computed by the solver already when the module is checked;
  - the instance of an attempt is picked by the generator from the attempt's seed (see the generator and the places of randomness below);
- builds the open view of a task — without the key, the solution and the traps — by the marks `x-open` in the format's schemas: a field without a mark is closed. It builds the open views on the server from the server part, and they are not part of the package;
- holds the check kinds and normalization: in format v0 these are `number`, `fraction`, `choice`, `predicate` and `rubric`. This is the platform's code, shared by all modules: a module only chooses the kind and sets its parameters;
- gives the verdict: compares the observation with the key or the predicate. A verdict is admitted only if the key or the predicate was given by the author, a solver or an adult, and on a task from paper also by the key of the AI's solution;
- joins the observations of two readers into one, by the slot check, and gives the verdict by the joined observation as a whole — in the reading mode `single`, by the one reader's, with nothing to join —, with the causes of "unreadable" from the observation ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md)). With the same code it gives the eval runner of `tutor` the verdict by the truth of a sample's markup;
- gives the key of a task from paper and the verdict by it ([contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md)): the solver computes the answer of the formula that AI read of the statement, exactly; an answer of the AI's solution is held to the rules of one before it is a key; and the readings of the work, brought to the key, are joined and judged by the package `observation` against the task's one item;
- holds the leak check — the search for the key and the leak phrases that the author named. `api` checks the tutor's text with it before the text goes to the device, and the module check checks the author's texts;
- executes Starlark in a sandbox, as in MathTrail's standalone, — in go.starlark.net of the version that the format names, and in one dialect for all the Starlark of the format:
  - check predicates — a count of steps that reaches 1,000,000, or an error, gives "unchecked";
  - template solvers — no more than 100,000,000 steps for all the instances of a template.

  The clock, concurrent runs and the version of the interpreter are under the Starlark sandbox below;
- checks a package by the chain of digests from the index that a pull request of `api` pinned: index → inventory → parts → files. The library checks no signatures: the signature of a release file is checked by `spec`'s recipe `release-verify` where the file enters NovaTrail;
- gives the author a CLI: check a module, build a package, build the index and compare versions — `diff` —, and to the build of the samples page of `canvas` it gives the open views and the media files by the command `open`: for a template, the instance by the seed, and for each task, the digest of its open view. The commands are under the library and the CLI below. The CLI builds the index inside the shared release of `modules`, after the number and before publication. "Build" writes the package as files, and the package's archive for the release is put by a recipe of `modules`. The withdrawals "build the index" reads from the file `withdrawals.yaml` of `modules` — by `$defs/withdrawals` of the index's schema ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)).

**Does not:**
- does not call AI. The work is read by `tutor`, and `module-kit` already gets an observation from `api`;
- does not go to the network: the library is computation without input or output, and the CLI only reads and writes the module's files. The build gets the previous index as a file: the shared release of `.github` downloads and checks it;
- stores no learners' data: of the platform's parts, only `api` stores them on the server;
- does not own the format. The schemas, the format text, the examples and the test vectors lie in `format/` of `spec`, and `module-kit` holds the format's semantics in code. CI of `spec` checks the examples with a JSON Schema validator, and the vectors with its second implementation, without `module-kit`: otherwise `spec` would depend on what depends on it;
- does not define the random generator and the derivation of seeds. `spec` defines them, with test vectors, and `module-kit` passes them. The generator is PCG from Go's `math/rand/v2`, and the places of randomness are derived as in standalone's simulator. They are one for the whole platform — for `module-kit`, the widgets, `engine` and `sim`;
- does not sign packages and the index and checks no signatures: the signature is put by the shared release workflow from `.github`, and checked by `spec`'s recipe `release-verify`;
- does not choose a lesson or a task and does not compute the learner model: that is `engine` and `learner-model`. Their types of the graph are simple and not from `module-kit`: `learner-model` defines them, and `engine` takes them from it;
- does not publish modules: the registry v0 is the signed index that CI of `modules` builds. The registry service will appear at stage 4 and will also be built on `module-kit`.

**Data and links:**
- it has no data of its own: it works with the module, the package and the index that it is given. There are no learners' data and no secrets, as in no repository. In the process of `api` it gets the observation and the key or the predicate of a task, and returns the verdict; on a task from paper, the formula and the answer of AI too, and it returns the key;
- at build it depends on nothing but `spec`:
  - the format's schemas, examples and vectors it takes as the Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec` — `spec.Format` at the tag of the submodule `spec/` —, not from the submodule;
  - the types of the parts, of their open views, of the canonical view, of the inventory and of the index — from the packages `types/formatv0` and `types/formatv0/open` of the same module;
  - the observation — the contract package `novatrail.observation.v1` of the nested Go module `github.com/altnovatrail/spec/contracts`, which `spec` generates from the API contracts, and the vectors of the join and of the verdict by an observation — `contracts.Vectors` of the same module. `tutor`, `module-kit` and `api` exchange the observation in one process, and it has one type. The module `contracts` requires the protobuf runtime and connect-go: these are third-party Go modules, not repositories. The package `observation` takes the protobuf runtime, BSD-3-Clause, and `ci-licenses` sees it, while connect-go does not go into the build.

  Both modules of `spec` move with the submodule, by a move to a tag, and not by Dependabot; how this is held is under code, checks and releases below;
- it is imported by `api` and the eval runner of `tutor`, and the CLI is taken at an exact tag by CI of `modules` and CI of `canvas` — as a release binary, which `release-verify` checks before the first run. CI of `canvas` takes the CLI to build the samples page: the page gets the open views and the media files at build, from the packages of the release of `modules`. For this the CLI has a command of its own, `open`. At stage 4 `module-registry` and `module-pipeline` are built on it;
- `sim` does not take it: it reads the server part by the types of the canonical view from `spec`. `learner-model` and `engine` read no packages: `api` passes them the graph;
- the verdict is reproducible. An attempt keeps the digest of its package — the digest of the inventory —, the module format version, the version of `module-kit` and the seed. The verdict is repeated from the stored observation, without AI, by the release of `module-kit` that gave it. A change of the behaviour of a check kind is a new version of the format;
- a new AI model is admitted by eval, and the false alarms there are counted by the verdict, through `module-kit`;
- CI runs the shared checks of `.github`, `docs-check-repo` among them, and the checks of its own code; which they are and what holds the merge is under code, checks and releases below.
  - The findings of CodeQL, Scorecard and SonarCloud are closed before a new task, by the rules of [RUN.md](../RUN.md), and the reasons of those that stay open are recorded once, for all repositories, in `spec`'s [decision log](../spec/docs/decisions.md).
  - The Go checks are recipes of its own, run by the shared `go.yml` of `.github`; CodeQL reads Go with the shared `codeql.yml`.
  - The release is put out by the shared `release.yml` of `.github`, and the files for it are built by a recipe of its own, `release-files`.
  - Code comes with its tests: the coverage goes to Codecov, and the merge waits for `codecov/patch` — at least 90% of the changed lines;
- stage 1, together with `modules`: the module format is tried at once on three modules of two subjects. The work in the order in which the repositories appear is led by the cross-repository plan, `spec/COORDINATION.md`.

## 2. To decide when detailed

The first four points below are the card's "To decide when detailed": not questions about the format, but what the format left to `module-kit`. Two more are beyond the card: the CLI command for open views, which `spec` leaves to this repository, and the type of the observation, what the code took from `spec`. Every point is decided: a point says what was decided and what `spec` answered of it, and the design is under Design below. Only the memory of third-party Starlark is put off on purpose. A question that goes beyond this repository goes «в реестр spec».

### 2.1. The module package and the canonical text

- **Gist:** how `module-kit` builds the module package that the format defines and what it writes its canonical text with. The contents of the package are the format's, and what is decided here is how to carry them out.
- **Decided:** the canonical text is written by a function of its own, which stops on a value outside the format, and the canonical view is built from the JSON value of each file as it is. How the package is built is under the module package below.

### 2.2. The generator in CI

- **Gist:** what `module-kit` executes the format's generator and places of randomness with — Go's standard library or its own code — and how its CI passes the vectors `format/vectors/random/` of `spec`.
- **Decided:** the generator is its own code by the constants of the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), with an error on an argument outside the domain, and `math/rand/v2` and `hash/fnv` are only the reference in the tests. The generator lies in `internal/`, and what goes outside is the choice of an attempt's instance. How it is built is under the generator and the places of randomness below.

### 2.3. The clock, concurrent runs and memory of Starlark

- **Gist:** how much time the evaluation of a check predicate and a template solver have, how many of them run at once, who holds the clock and the slots, what a clock that fired means, how much memory the sandbox has and whether a separate process is needed. The steps, the dialect and the solver's place are the format's, and the clock and concurrent runs it leaves to `module-kit`.
- **Decided:** go.starlark.net of the version that the format names, pinned in `go.mod`, and the dialect, one for all the Starlark of the format, the predicate's too, the format names itself. The clock is a safeguard of `module-kit`: 1 s for a predicate and 60 s for a template solver. The library holds no slots: concurrency is the caller's, in `api` no more than `GOMAXPROCS` at once. How it is built is under the Starlark sandbox below.
- **Put off:** the memory of third-party Starlark. From stage 4 the predicates in `api` and the solvers in `module-registry` are written by third-party authors too, and go.starlark.net does not count memory. From stage 4 the Starlark of a third-party module is executed in a separate process with a memory limit, and how is decided by the tasks of `api` and `module-registry` (О-9). Until then — Д10 of [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md).

### 2.4. The semver rules for `diff`

- **Gist:** by what rules `module-kit` compares a new version of a module with the published ones: what raises the major, the minor and the patch version, what the comparison finds itself, and what the author declares. The card names the comparison among its points: a new version is compared with each published one below it by semver, withdrawn ones too.
- **Decided:** the comparison itself finds the lowest version part, and the author may raise a higher one, but not a lower one. Major — when something of what the learner had in the previous version is gone or means something else. Packages are compared, and the CLI has a command of its own for the comparison, `diff` — under version comparison below.
- **spec's answer:** the principle of the parts, the table "change — version part", the rule of 0.y.z and the rules of comparison are the format's, in the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), with the vectors `vectors/versions/`, and the IDs of the rules are `module-version.*`. A new version is compared with each published one, and a package of a previous format version is read by the places that the format keeps in all versions. Where the format differs from what this repository decided, version comparison below says.

### 2.5. A CLI command for open views

- **Gist:** whether the CLI of `module-kit` needs a command of its own that gives the open views of tasks to the build of the samples page of `canvas`, or whether the card's commands are enough for that build — check, build, build the index. `spec` leaves the question to this repository: the CLI is part of `module-kit`.
- **Decided:** a command of its own, `open`, at a release of `modules`. It writes the file of open views — for each module, by its newest unwithdrawn version, the ID, the version, the package digest, the open views of the manifest and of the tasks, as in `tutor-open-tasks`, — and the media files of the tasks, `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`, checked against the inventory. Withdrawn versions it skips. How it is built is under the library and the CLI below.
- **spec's answer:** what `open` gives for a template and how the digest of an open view is computed is the eval protocol's. For a template — the instance by the seed, and the digest of an open view is `sha256:` and the SHA-256 of its canonical text, for an instance — with the numbers substituted.

### 2.6. The type of the observation

- **Gist:** `module-kit` gives the verdict by the observation — what `tutor` read in the work. `tutor`, `module-kit` and `api` exchange the observation in one process, and it has one type — code that `spec` generates from the contracts into the nested Go module `spec/contracts`.
- **spec's answer:** the contract of the observation is the package `novatrail.observation.v1` of the module `spec/contracts`. `module-kit` joins two readings into one, by the slot check, and gives the verdict by the joined observation; how, the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md) says, and the vectors are `contracts.Vectors`. The module format does not change from this: the verdict by the read value stays by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md).
- **Decided:** the package `observation` over `verdict` — `Validate`, `Join`, `Verdicts` and `Essay`. An invalid observation is the error `ErrNotValid`, so `unchecked` in a verdict by a valid observation is the `unchecked` of a predicate. `verdict` stays without protobuf. How it is built is under the library and the CLI below.

## 3. Design

How the component is built, once what is still to decide above is decided: the code, its checks and releases, the module package, the generator, the Starlark sandbox, version comparison, and the library and the CLI as a whole.

### 3.1. Code, checks and releases

- **The module** is `github.com/altnovatrail/module-kit`. The `go` line of `go.mod` is the lower bound of Go for those who import it. The Go that builds and checks it is `ARG GO_VERSION` of the stage `toolchain-core` of `.devcontainer/Dockerfile`, and so of `toolchain`, which is built from it, where `GOTOOLCHAIN=local`: the Go of `spec` itself.
- **The modules of `spec`** are `github.com/altnovatrail/spec` and `github.com/altnovatrail/spec/contracts`, at the version that `go.mod` names, the tag of the submodule `spec/`; the nested module's tag, `contracts/` with the same version, is at the same commit. Dependabot `gomod` does not raise them — `ignore` by name —, and they are moved by a move to a tag together with the submodule. `ci-lint` checks that the commit recorded for `spec/` in the git index is the origin of the version of each module at the Go proxy, and that the version of `contracts` is the same as that of `spec`. The packages of `spec` and `contracts` import nothing outside the standard library. The contract package `novatrail.observation.v1`, `observationv1` of the module `contracts`, is taken by `observation`: it imports the protobuf runtime, and it does not import connect-go.
- **Third-party modules**, each at the version of `go.mod`:

- **The container** is the one that the template of `spec` gives a repository of the kind `go`: the container of `spec` and gopls in the stage `devcontainer`. `.devcontainer/` is what the template's generator `tools/new-repo/` gives for the kind `go` at the tag of the submodule, taken afresh by a move to a tag, but for a change of the template's comments alone, which waits for the next change of `.devcontainer/` beyond comments. ### 3.2. The module package

The CLI builds the package at a release of `modules`, from the module's folder, by the format version that its manifest names ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). A package is built only from a module that has passed the check: the schema and all the format rules. The code: `modulepackage.Build(ctx, fsys)` first checks the module with `module.Check` and rejects a module with breaches by a `*Refusal` with the report, and returns the package as the bytes of its files, `Package.Files`, which the CLI writes to disk; `modulepackage.Read(fsys, digest)` reads a package as files by the chain of digests; the canonical text is `internal/canonical.Text`.

- **The parts of the package**:
  - the server part is the canonical view, `server.json`: the module as one JSON object, with all its content, and with the keys, the solutions and the traps too;
  - the device part is `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and the media files, and, once the format has widgets, their bundles. It is named by its listing, `device.json`;
  - the inventory is `inventory.json`: the format version, the module's ID and version, and both parts by their digests and sizes. It is not the module manifest, although in code and schemas the manifest is called `manifest`.
- **How it is built:**
  1. The YAML reader gives the JSON value of each file of a part. Files and folders whose names begin with `.`, comments and `eval/` do not go into the package.
  2. The canonical view: the parts of the module are under members of their own, `manifest`, `skill_graph`, `traps`, `techniques` and `solving_steps`, and `tasks` and `lessons` as objects by local ID. The value of a part is the JSON value of its file as it is: a template as it is written, and no default values. Beyond the parts, there are `skill_tiers`, the tier of each skill, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), when the module has a skill graph, and `instances`, the numbers and the solver's results of each instance of a template, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), when any of the tasks are templates. A member without its part — `skill_tiers` without a skill graph, and `instances` without templates — is absent from the view. An integer that the YAML reader gave as a double — `50.0` or `5e1` — goes into the view as the integer `50` ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)).
  3. The listing of the device part is an object: `license` — the digest and size of `LICENSE`; `notice` — of `NOTICE`, only if it exists; `media` — of each file of `media/` by its path from `media/`, only if there are such files (`inventory.schema.json`, `$defs/device_listing`).
  4. The inventory: `module_format_version`, `id` and `version` as in the manifest, and `server_part` and `device_part` — the digest and size of the canonical text of the canonical view and of the listing.
  5. The package digest is the digest of the canonical text of the inventory. A package as files is a folder: `inventory.json`, `server.json` and `device.json` — the canonical texts of the inventory, the canonical view and the listing — and `device/` with `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and `media/<path>` byte for byte. So "build" writes them, and the package's archive for the release is put by a recipe of `modules`.

  The package depends only on the module's files and the format version of its manifest: it holds no version of `module-kit`, no time and no paths.
- **The canonical text** is written by a function of its own over a tree of JSON values — object, array, string, integer, `true`, `false`, `null`:
  - the members of an object go in the order of the UTF-16 code units of their names, and there is no whitespace between tokens;
  - an integer is written in decimal digits, a negative one with the sign `-`;
  - in a string only `"` and `\`, `\b`, `\t`, `\n`, `\f` and `\r` are escaped, and the other characters up to U+001F are written as `\u00` and two lowercase hexadecimal digits. `<`, `>`, `&`, U+2028 and U+2029 are written as they are, and characters are not normalized;
  - the text is UTF-8, with no BOM and no newline at the end;
  - on a number with a fractional part, an integer outside ±(2^53−1), a string that is not valid UTF-8 and a value that is not JSON, the function stops with an error. On a checked module such a refusal means a defect in the code of `module-kit`. Only it writes the texts of the package and of the module index: not `encoding/json`, not `jsontext` and not a walk over the types of `formatv0`.
- **A digest** is `sha256:` and 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits of the bytes, and a size is the number of the bytes. The attempt keeps the package digest.
- **Reading a package** goes by the chain of digests from the package digest that the index pinned: inventory → parts → files. The library checks no signatures. Beyond the digests, `Read` holds the format version of the inventory, the schema and the canonical text of each of the three texts, and that the manifest of the view names the module of the inventory; a refusal is `ErrChain`, `ErrFormatVersion` or `ErrNotCanonical`, with the file's path. JSON is read by `canonical.Parse`, shared with the index: a text not in UTF-8 and a number outside double, such as `1e700`, are not JSON, `ErrNotCanonical`, and never reach the schema validator. `ReadParts` is the same reading without the files of `device/`: so version comparison reads a published package, which CI of `modules` gives without the device part, and compares that part by its listing.
- **Who reads the package:**
  - through `module-kit`: `api`, by the digest, and the eval runner of `tutor`, and from stage 4 `module-registry`;
  - through the CLI of `module-kit`: CI of `canvas`, for the samples page;
  - without `module-kit`: `sim`, by the types of the canonical view from `spec`;
  - the device part is served by `api`.
- **Where it lies:** at stage 1 the packages and the index lie in the releases of `modules`; at stage 2 the packages lie in the image of `api` too. To repeat a verdict, old versions of packages are taken from the releases of `modules` (Т-17).
- **Tests:**
  - all the vectors of `format/vectors/packages/` and `format/vectors/tiers/` from `spec.Format`. CI computes the solver's results of the vector `with-solver` by a run of its own;
  - unit tests of the escaping table and of each refusal;
  - a fuzz test that compares the function with `jsontext.Value.Canonicalize` on values that the format allows. It lies in a file with `//go:build goexperiment.jsonv2`, and with `GOEXPERIMENT=nojsonv2` the other tests run without it;
  - in the code: the vectors of `packages/` and `tiers/` — the runner `packages` of the vector test; `TestEscapes`, `TestEveryControlCharacter`, `TestText`, `TestRefusals`, `TestDigest` and `FuzzText` in `internal/canonical`; `TestBuildFiles`, `TestBuildLeavesOut`, `TestBuildRefuses`, `TestBuildContext`, `TestIntegers`, `TestRead`, `TestReadRefuses` and `FuzzRead` in `modulepackage`, and the parsing of JSON, `canonical.Parse`, shared with the index — `TestParse` in `internal/canonical`.

### 3.3. The generator and the places of randomness

`module-kit` executes the generator and the places of randomness by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md), with its own code.

- **The Go package** is `internal/random`: it is not part of the library's API, and other Go modules do not import it. The generator is one for the platform by its algorithm, not by its code: `engine`, `sim` and the widgets execute it themselves and pass the same vectors.
- **The package's API**: `New(high, low)` — a stream from two words; `Words(seed, names)` — the words of a place, and `Place(seed, names...)` — its stream; `Stream` has `Integer`, `Below`, `Between`, `Fraction` and `Permutation`, one function of the format per method, and `MaxPermutation` is 2^31 − 1. A refusal of a function and of a place is `ErrDomain`, with a message saying what is wrong.
- **A stream** is two words of state, high and low, taken as they are. A step is `state · M + C mod 2^128` through `bits.Mul64` and `bits.Add64`, the output is DXSM, and the constants are the numbers from the format text. There is no shared state: a stream lives within one call.
- **A place of randomness** is a seed, an unsigned integer of 64 bits, and a list of names. Its stream is started by two words: FNV-1a 64 of the UTF-8 text "the seed in decimal digits, then `|` and the name, for each name", and the seed itself. There is at least one name, and a name is a non-empty string of valid UTF-8 without `|`; otherwise the place is not built, and an error is returned. In the same way, on `math/rand/v2` and `hash/fnv`, standalone's simulator derives the stream `seeded` — the place of the master seed with the names "experiment, unit, purpose" —, while the production code of standalone has no generator from a seed ([standalone@c6cbeb2 `internal/`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/tree/c6cbeb2/internal), [`tools/learners/child.go`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/tools/learners/child.go)).
- **The functions** are five, those that the vectors check. Each draws as many integers as the format text says:

  | Function | Domain | How it computes |
  |---|---|---|
  | `integer` | — | the next integer of the stream |
  | `below(b)` | b from 1 to 2^64 − 1 | a power of two by a mask; otherwise Lemire's method: the high word of the product of the integer and b, with rejection while the low word is less than (2^64 − b) mod b |
  | `between(a, c)` | a ≤ c and c − a + 1 ≤ 2^63 − 1. First a ≤ c is checked, and then, without overflow, that `uint64(c) − uint64(a)` is at most 2^63 − 2 | a + `below(c − a + 1)` |
  | `fraction` | — | the low 53 bits of the next integer, divided by 2^53 |
  | `permutation(n)` | n from 0 to 2^31 − 1 | from 0, 1, …, n − 1 in order, and for i from n − 1 down to 1, a swap of the places i and `below(i + 1)` |

  On an argument outside its domain a function returns an error and draws no integers. There is no shuffle of a list: it has no vectors, and `module-kit` shuffles nothing.
- **The instance of an attempt** is one function of the templates API, `template.AttemptInstance(seed, task, count)`: the number `below(count)` of the place of the attempt's seed with the names `template`, the task's ID and `instance`, for count from 1 to 10,000, `template.MaxInstances` ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). The number counts from 0, in the order of the counter, and count is the number of instances whose numbers the package carries: the function takes no template and counts no instances. A count outside the domain and an ID that is not a name of a place give `template.ErrDomain`. No other call is made on this place, and a module has no other places in format v0. A task's ID is lowercase Latin letters, digits and `-` (`common.schema.json`), and a place's name from it is always allowed. `api` draws the attempt's seed from a cryptographic source, and `module-kit` gets it as a number. Neither the seed nor the words of the place go to the device: the words show the seed ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)).
- **What the code does not have:** randomness not from a seed — neither `math/rand`, nor the top-level functions of `math/rand/v2`, nor `crypto/rand`. `math/rand/v2` and `hash/fnv` are only in the tests.
- **Tests:**
  - all the vectors of `format/vectors/random/` from `spec.Format`. A vector is read by the YAML reader and the schema validator of the module check — against `random-vector.schema.json` — and by the type `formatv0.RandomVector`, and the numbers written as strings are parsed by `strconv`. When a seed and names are given, the derived words are compared with `state`. Then the calls go in order, and each value is compared, `fraction` as a double. The test fails if there are no vectors and if a call has no known field;
  - the place of a template is checked by the vectors of `format/vectors/templates/` too: for each seed they name the instance. The instance number of the 16 seeds of the five vectors without a solver is held by `TestAttemptInstanceOfTheVectors` in `template`, together with `Instances` at that number giving the parameters of the attempt, and the vectors as a whole are passed by the vector test;
  - unit tests: each refusal and the number of integers that each function draws: `below(1)` and `between(a, a)` draw one, `permutation(0)` and `permutation(1)` none (`TestWords`, `TestDraws`, `TestRefusals`, `TestEdges`);
  - the comparison with the standard library: the stream, the functions and the words of a place are compared with `math/rand/v2` and `hash/fnv`, and the state of the stream after each function with `PCG.MarshalBinary`. An ordinary test, `TestReference`, runs on every `go test` — on the words of all the vectors, on the bounds 1, powers of two, 2^63 − 1, 2^63 + 1 and 2^64 − 1, and on 5,000 inputs from the generator itself with a constant seed. A fuzz test, `FuzzReference`, compares the same on any inputs, with permutations of up to 1024 numbers, and the same inputs, except the inputs from the generator, lie in its corpus. The reference lives only in the tests, and they need no build tag.

### 3.4. The Starlark sandbox

The Starlark of the format — check predicates, template expressions and solvers — `module-kit` executes in go.starlark.net, in the caller's process. The code is the package `internal/sandbox`.

- **Limits:**

  | Limit | What it holds | Who holds it | Where the number comes from |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | a predicate — no longer than 1000 characters | the size of the expression | the module check, by the schema | the format |
  | a predicate — the count of steps does not reach 1,000,000 | the steps of one evaluation in the count of the pinned version of go.starlark.net | `Thread.SetMaxExecutionSteps`: the count reached the limit — "unchecked" | the format |
  | the clock of a predicate — 1 s | the time of an evaluation whose steps call heavy built-in functions | `module-kit`, a constant of the release; when it fires — "unchecked" | a predicate spends 6 ms on 1,000,000 steps of brute force (measurements 1.1) |
  | a template expression — no longer than 1000 characters, no more steps than tokens; combinations — no more than 10,000 per template | the cost of the brute force over the instances | the module check: the length — by the schema, the combinations — by the rule `task.template-instances`; an expression has no limit of steps and no clock | the format |
  | a solver — no longer than 8192 characters | the size of the program | the module check, by the schema | the format |
  | a solver — the count of steps does not reach 100,000,000 per template | the steps of the top level and of all the calls of `solve` in the same count | `Thread.SetMaxExecutionSteps` on the one thread of the template: the count reached the limit — a breach of `task.template-solver` | the format |
  | the clock of a solver — 60 s per template | the time of a program whose steps call heavy built-in functions | `module-kit`, a constant of the release; when it fires — a breach of `task.template-solver` | a simple loop spends 0.45 s on 100,000,000 steps (measurements 1.1) |
  | recursion is off | the Go stack from the recursion of Starlark functions: its overflow crashes the whole process | the dialect, `Recursion: false`; go.starlark.net catches recursion through `lambda` too. Built-in functions on deeply nested values this does not hold — they, like memory, are held only by a separate process | the format, for a solver and for a predicate |
  | concurrent runs | the CPU of the process and the wall time of the clock | the caller: `api` — no more than `GOMAXPROCS` at once, the CLI — a solver per CPU | the clock runs by wall time, and "unchecked" must not depend on the load |
  | a panic of the interpreter | the caller's process | `module-kit`, `recover`: "unchecked" or a breach of `task.template-solver` | #672 of go.starlark.net |
  | memory | the caller's process | until stage 4 — nobody: go.starlark.net does not count it. From stage 4 the Starlark of a third-party module — in a separate process with a memory limit at the caller | Д10; О-9 |
  | the version of go.starlark.net | the count of steps and the behaviour of the language | the format names it, and `module-kit` pins it in `go.mod` and excludes it from Dependabot `gomod` | the format |

  The steps are counted by go.starlark.net of the version that `module-kit` pinned: the counter grows by 10 per backward jump or call ([starlark-go@4f4267f `interp.go`](https://github.com/google/starlark-go/blob/4f4267f/starlark/interp.go)). The same module on the same values makes the same steps on any machine, but not in another version of the interpreter: so the format names the version, and its change is a major version of the format. The limit fires when the count reaches it ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)).
- **The call.** An evaluation runs synchronously, in the caller's thread, on its own `starlark.Thread` and with the caller's `context`. The clock of `module-kit` and the cancellation of the `context` call `Thread.Cancel` with different reasons. The first gives "unchecked" or a breach of a rule, the second an error without a verdict. The interpreter sees a cancellation between instructions, and does not interrupt a built-in function: in the measurement `len(list(range(50000000)))` stopped only after the clock, having taken hundreds of mebibytes, and `str` of a deep list ran on one step much longer than the clock (measurements 1.1). The clock runs by wall time, so the caller holds no more than `GOMAXPROCS` concurrent evaluations. The sandbox has no slots and no shared state.
- **The dialect** is one for all the Starlark of the format, as in the [format text](../spec/format/README.md): `Set`, `While`, `TopLevelControl` and `GlobalReassign` are on, `Recursion` is off, `load` is forbidden, and the built-in names are the 33 names of `starlark.Universe` of the pinned version that the format lists. A check predicate and template expressions go in the same dialect. The sandbox holds the names by a list of its own, not by `starlark.Universe`: the caller's process may add its own to it, and the dialect does not change from this.
- **The code** — `internal/sandbox`:
  - `CheckExpr(src, names...)` — an expression of the dialect that names only `names`, the names that it binds itself and the built-in names: the parsing by `syntax.FileOptions.ParseExpr` and the names by `resolve.ExprOptions` with a list of its own. An error is the line, the column and the words of go.starlark.net: `1:1: undefined: number`. With it `verdict.CheckPredicate` holds the rule `task.predicate-expression`;
  - `Eval(ctx, limits, src, env)` — the same `CheckExpr` on the names of `env`, then the parsing again and `starlark.EvalExprOptions` on its own thread, as the vectors count steps. `Limits{Steps, Clock}`, zero — no limit, and `Predicate` — 1,000,000 steps and 1 s. The stops are `ErrSteps`, `ErrClock` and `ErrPanic`, and a cancellation is the error of the `context`, wrapped; the error names the first reason, as the thread keeps the first reason of `Cancel`, and only when the interpreter stopped by it: an evaluation that ended by itself keeps its result and its error. `print` writes nothing, and the thread has no `Load`;
  - `Compile(src, names...)` — the same `CheckExpr`, and then a program of one assignment `_result = <src>`, built from the tree of the expression, where the names are predeclared: it compiles everything that `CheckExpr` takes, is as deep for the parsing of go.starlark.net as the expression itself, and makes the same steps as `Eval`. `Func.Call(ctx, limits, args...)` — `Program.Init` with the values of the names in order on its own thread, with the same limits, stops and errors as `Eval`. So the template expressions are computed on each combination without parsing again: without a limit of steps and a clock, because the steps of an expression of the subset are bounded by its length;
  - `CheckProgram(src)` — a program of the dialect: the parsing with the options of the dialect, not a single `load`, and the names — a list of its own, as for `CheckExpr`. `Exec(ctx, limits, src)` — `CheckProgram`, the compilation and the top level of the program on its own thread, after which the global values are frozen, as Starlark freezes a module: so is a solver's `Program`. `Program.Call(fn, args...)` calls its function on the same thread, and `Program.Close()` ends its clock and the watching of the `context`: the steps of the top level and of all the calls are one count against one limit, and the clock is one — from `Exec` to `Close`. `Solver` — 100,000,000 steps and 60 s. The stops and errors are the same as for `Eval`; after a panic `Call` calls nothing, and after `Close` it returns `ErrClosed`. The error of a `context` that the caller made done is recognized by `ContextDone` — both the verdict of a predicate and the solver take it;
  - the thread is a type of its own inside the package: the limit of steps, the clock, the watching of the `context`, `recover` and the first reason of the stop — one code for one evaluation, `Eval` and `Func.Call`, and for a program with all its calls. The thread sees a cancellation at the entry into each Starlark function, at the return of each call and at a backward jump, so a clock that fired between calls of `solve` stops the next one at its entry.
- **The address reserve.** The import of `go.starlark.net/starlark` on 64-bit linux and darwin reserves 4 GiB of address space for small integers, and if that fails, it writes to `log` and computes more slowly ([starlark-go@4f4267f `int_posix64.go`](https://github.com/google/starlark-go/blob/4f4267f/starlark/int_posix64.go)). Every process that imports `verdict` inherits this — `api` and the eval runner of `tutor`: the reserve takes no memory, but a limit of the process's address space sees it.
- **The CLI** checks template solvers one per CPU (`GOMAXPROCS`), each on its own Starlark thread, and prints the result in the order of the templates: `template.Instances` is a function for one template, and it may be called from several goroutines at once. The pool is held by the check itself: `module.Check(ctx, fsys)` expands the templates of the module in `runtime.GOMAXPROCS(0)` goroutines, each takes the next template in the order of the paths, and the result takes its place by the task's path, so the report does not depend on the number of goroutines. The `context` is the caller's; in the CLI, SIGINT and SIGTERM make it done. `modulepackage.Build` takes the numbers of the instances from the check, `module.CheckModule`, and the solver runs once. Only `CheckFile`, a file on its own, runs with `context.Background()` and one at a time. The steps of a thread do not depend on its neighbours. Wall time and memory are shared by the solvers: heavy built-in functions of several templates at once reach the clock or the memory limit sooner than one at a time.
- **What is known from standalone:**
  - 25,000,000 steps and 2 s per run, one run per vCPU and 3 s of waiting for a slot; the limit of steps was calibrated on 450 reference solvers, and the worst needed 2,081,362 steps ([standalone@c6cbeb2 `SPEC.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md));
  - its helpers pay a step for every 16 bytes and build no lists longer than 1,000,000 elements: so the steps bound the memory of the helpers too — 381 MiB at the ceiling of steps —, and nine tenths of the memory of a service instance are given to the Go runtime, `GOMEMLIMIT` ([standalone@c6cbeb2 `SPEC.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md));
  - but the memory gap is wider than one operator. The operator `*` and the repeated `xs = xs + xs` build a huge list past the counters; the literal `{}` in a loop holds 512 bytes for every nine steps, and a service instance with 1 GiB of memory was killed exactly so; the methods of the values themselves — `x.extend(range(3000000))` — take three million elements in nineteen steps ([standalone@c6cbeb2 `SPEC.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/SPEC.md), [standalone@c6cbeb2 `docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/docs/decisions.md));
  - standalone v1 put off a separate process, and rejected four slots with a safety clock: "Verdicts depend on the load" ([standalone@c6cbeb2 `docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/mathtrail-standalone/blob/c6cbeb2/docs/decisions.md));
  - the steps of the heaviest of its solvers on the version of standalone and on 4f4267fb140d — measurements 1.1.
- **Put off:** the memory of third-party Starlark from stage 4 — among what is still to decide, with the clock and concurrent runs of Starlark.

### 3.5. Version comparison

A new version of a module `module-kit` compares with each published version, withdrawn ones too, by the format's rules of version comparison: the principle of the parts, the table "change — version part", the rule of 0.y.z, the comparison of a check by meaning, the instances of a template by the values of the parameters and the eight rules `module-version.*` — [format text](../spec/format/README.md). `module-kit` has no rules and no table of its own: they are the format's, and the vectors `vectors/versions/` hold them as they hold the verdict. The version of a module is semver `X.Y.Z` without pre-releases and build metadata; it is not the format version, MAJOR.MINOR, which `module_format_version` names.

- **Where the format differs from this repository's own decision on comparison**, the format holds:
  - the IDs of the rules have the prefix `module-version.`, not names by parts: `module-version.above`, `module-version.step`, `module-version.retired-complete`, `module-version.retired-kept`, `module-version.retired-known`, `module-version.ids-kept` and `module-version.languages-kept`, and the eighth, `module-version.bands-kept`, holds the bands: the published bands stand first, in order and with the same IDs, and a new one only after the last;
  - the same version with another digest is a refusal of `module-version.above`, not of `module-index.one-package`; the index rule `module-index.version-above` holds the same at the build of the index ([format text](../spec/format/README.md));
  - a new entry of `retired_skills` is a major change, together with the retirement, and an entry is only expanded: a wrong one cannot be fixed;
  - a part also has "none": nothing has changed but the version, or the new version is the newest published one as it is;
  - a new version is compared with each published one, and CI of `modules` gives `diff` the package of each;
  - across a format transition the rules hold by the places of the package that the format keeps in all versions, and the part, while the format is v0, is major ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). Skills and IDs across a transition `diff` checks the same way as within one format version.
- **What is compared** are packages, not the author's YAML: the canonical view and the listing of the device part. The new version is the module's folder, which `diff` checks and builds in memory, or a package already built by `build`. The published versions are packages as files, each checked by the chain of digests from the index. A package of a previous format version is only `inventory.json` and `server.json`, which `diff` reads by the places that the format keeps in all versions. What does not go into the package — comments, the order of keys, `eval/`, files whose names begin with `.` — does not change the version: the package is the same.
- **The command `diff`:**

  ```
  module-kit diff [--json] (--index <module-index.json> | --first) [--published <package folder>]... <module>
  ```

  - `<module>` — a module's folder or a package's folder; they are told apart by `manifest.yaml` or `inventory.json` at the root. A module's folder `diff` checks and builds, as `check` and `build` do, and writes nowhere; a package built by `build` spares CI a second check with solvers;
  - `--index` — `module-index.json`, which CI has already checked by the recipe `release-verify`: the CLI checks no signatures. It reads the index by the places that the format keeps in all versions ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)), — `moduleindex.Read`, — and takes from it all the versions of the module and their format versions. `--first` says explicitly that there are no published versions, like the build of an index without a previous one: a missing file will not pass silently;
  - `--published` — the folders of packages as files ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)), without `device/`. `diff` recognizes a package by the digest of the bytes of `inventory.json` — this is the package digest —, and then checks `server.json` and, in the current format version, `device.json` against the inventory. Extra folders it skips, and the package of each version that the index names is needed: without it no comparison is possible;
  - code 0 — the version is accepted, the module has not changed, or this is the first version: `--first` or an index that does not name the module; 1 — a refusal: a line for each breached rule, `refused: <ID>: <words>`, or the report of the module check, as from `check`, if the check rejected the module; 2 — no comparison is possible: the call, input or output, a broken chain of digests, no package of a version that the index names;
  - the report: the module, the new version, with which versions it was compared, the part found, the allowed next versions and the raised part. Then the changes, a line each: the version part, the path — a JSON Pointer in the canonical view or in the listing of the device part —, and what changed; for instances — the values of the parameters. With `--json` — the same report as data;
  - the CLI does not go to the network: the index and the packages are put by CI of `modules`.
- **The library** is one function over checked packages — the new one and the published ones — and the index; the CLI wraps it, and from stage 4 `module-registry` calls it. The same "same major" is read by `api` when it moves a learner to a new version.
- **The code** — the package `moduleversion`:
  - `Compare(next, published)`: `next` — the `*modulepackage.Package` of the new version, `published` — `[]Published{Digest, Files}`, packages as files without `device/`. Each is read by `modulepackage.ReadParts` by the chain of digests — the inventory, `server.json` and `device.json`, without the files of the device part. The newest — by semver, withdrawn ones too; the same version with the same digest is `Unchanged`, and nothing is compared; without published ones — the first version. An error is a package that `ReadParts` did not accept, a package of a newer format version — `ErrFormatVersion` —, a package of another module and two packages of one version;
  - `Changes(before, after)` — the part and the changes of two packages of one module and one format version. Each row of the table "change — version part" of the [format text](../spec/format/README.md) is at its own place, as in `versions.mjs` of `spec`, and everything else, except `manifest.version`, is a patch at each place where the JSON values of `server.json` and `device.json` diverged: by members and elements, and the `instances` of a template as a whole. A difference at the place of a table row or under it is not written, and under `skill_tiers` and `instances/<ID>` — when the skill graph or the task itself diverged. A check is compared by meaning through `normal.Compared` and `normal.Units` — `verdict` calls them too; answer slots — by their place in the item, `answer_slots/<j>` on a sheet and `line/<k>` in a workbook; the instances of a template — by the numbers of the parameters, through `template.Instance` of each version;
  - `Steps`, `StepPart` and `SameMajor` — the rule of 0.y.z: the next versions after a version and the part of each step, the part of the step to a version — `None` if it is not a next one, — and whether two versions have the same major, as `diff` and `api` read it. The numbers are raised by `semver.Version.Raise`, as text and without a bound;
  - the report — `Comparison{Module, Version, Published, Newest, Unchanged, Part, Next, Raised, Changes}`: `Next` — the next versions whose step raises a part no lower than the one found, `Raised` — the part of the step to the new version. `String()` writes the line of the module and the versions, the part found, the next versions and the raised part, and then a change per line — like a breach of the module check: `server.json #/tasks/apples: minor: the task apples is added`, for an instance — `on the instance a = 2, b = 3:` before the words. The changes go by part from major, then by place: `server.json` before `device.json`, and within a file — `schema.ComparePointers`. With `--json` — the same `Comparison` through `encoding/json`: `module`, `version`, `published`, `newest`, `unchanged`, `version_part`, `next_versions`, `raised` and `changes`, for a change — `part`, `file`, `pointer`, `parameters` and `what`, and a part — by its word;
  - the rules — `Comparison.Breaches`, a `Breach{Rule, Message}` per breach, and `Refused()`; `Rules` — the eight IDs in the order of the [format text](../spec/format/README.md). `module-version.above` — for each published version, withdrawn ones too; `module-version.step` — for the newest, only if `above` let it pass, by `Raised` and `Part`; the other six read only the places that the format keeps in all versions — the languages, the IDs of the bands in order, the keys of skills, tasks, lessons, traps and techniques, `retired_skills` with `replaced_by`, — so they hold across a format transition too. The report writes each breach as a line `refused: <ID>: <words>` before the changes, and `--json` as `breaches`. The words are as in `versions.mjs` of `spec`;
  - a format transition: a published package whose inventory names an earlier format version is read by the chain — `inventory.json` by its digest, `server.json` by the digest and size from the inventory — and only by places: `Changes` does not compare it, and the part is major, while the format is v0, by one change of `inventory.json #/module_format_version`. A package of a newer format version is `ErrFormatVersion`: the format has not yet promised its places to a reader of this version. A package without a place that the format keeps is `ErrNotCanonical`;
  - the report is printed by the command `diff` — `cmd/module-kit/diff.go` and `published.go`: the versions of the module — from `moduleindex.Read`, the package of each — among `--published` by the `package_digest` of the entry, `moduleversion.Published{Digest, Files}` from the folder; with `--json`, for a module that the check rejected — `{"check": [...]}` with the paths from the folder of the command line.
- **Tests:**
  - all the vectors of `format/vectors/versions/` from `spec.Format` — the part found or a refusal with the rule's ID;
  - in the code: the runner `versions` of the vector test — the cases with a part within one format version, the refusals, each by its own rule and only by it, and the format transitions; `TestPartsAreTheFormats`, `TestSteps`, `TestStepPart`, `TestSameMajor`, `TestChanges` — the places and the words on eight vectors, — `TestCompare`, `TestComparisonString`, `TestRulesAreTheFormats`, `TestBreaches` — the words of each rule, the format transition, `earlier-format-older-version` and `newest-unchanged`, — `TestReadPublished`, `TestPlacesOf` and `FuzzDiff` in `moduleversion`, `TestRaise` in `internal/semver`, `TestCompared` in `internal/normal`, `TestOrderOfPointers` in `internal/schema` and `TestReadParts` in `modulepackage`;
  - unit tests of the exit codes and of the report: the module has not changed, no package of a version from the index, a broken chain, `--first`, a package of a previous format version; `TestDiffVectors` — all the vectors of `versions/` through the command, with the index and the packages of the published versions, as folders; `TestDiffCases` — the first version by `--first` and by an index without the module, the new version as a package, a module that the check rejected, an extra folder among `--published`; `TestDiffJSON` — the report with `--json`, codes 0 and 1; `TestDiffFails` — the cases of code 2.

  - **the key of a task from paper** — the package `paper`: the solver's answer of a formula or why it is not taken, the rule an answer of AI breaks, the key, and the item and the values brought by which `observation` gives the verdict, below;
  - **the leak check** — the package `leak`: the keys and leak phrases of a task that were found in a text, each with its place in the task. `api` builds the search on the instance of the attempt and searches the tutor's words with it, and a template is an error, below;
  - **the module check, the package build, the index build and version comparison** — for the CLI, and from stage 4 for `module-registry` too.
- **The CLI's commands.** They all share one meaning of the exit code: 0 — accepted, 1 — a refusal by the format rules, with the ID of each rule broken, 2 — the command could not do its work: the call, input or output, a broken chain of digests. What is absent — a previous index, withdrawals — a command is told explicitly, not by a missing file. The CLI does not go to the network.

  | Command | What it reads | What it writes | Who calls it |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `check <module>` | the module's folder | the report: the breaches with the rules' IDs, and the warnings — of a number in a text of the whole module and of a picture with transparent pixels | CI of `modules` |
  | `build <module> <folder>` | the module's folder, which has passed the check | the package as files: `inventory.json`, `server.json`, `device.json` and `device/`. The archive for the release is put by a recipe of `modules` | CI and the release of `modules` |
  | `index` | the previous index or an explicit "no previous one", the release's package folders, its tag, and the withdrawals — the file `withdrawals.yaml` by `$defs/withdrawals` of the index's schema, or an explicit "no withdrawals" | `module-index.json`, the canonical text of the index; a refusal, with the ID of the rule of the index | the recipe `release-files` of `modules`, inside the shared release |
  | `diff` | a module or a package, a checked index or `--first`, the packages of the published versions | the version part found and the changes, by the rules of version comparison above | CI of `modules` |
  | `open` | the index of a release of `modules`, the index of the newest release — for the marks of withdrawal —, the seeds of templates and the packages | `open-views.json` and the files of the device part (below) | CI of `canvas`, for the samples page |

  This section is the form of each call for the recipes of `modules` and CI of `canvas`.
- **The commands `check` and `build`** — `cmd/module-kit`, `main.go` and `build.go`:
  - **the call** — `module-kit check <module>` and `module-kit build <module> <folder>`. Any other call: usage to stderr and code 2. SIGINT and SIGTERM make the command's `context` done, and the solvers stop, with code 2;
  - **the report** — to stdout, a line per breach: `<path>[:line:column] [#/pointer]: <rule ID>: <message>`, the path from the folder that the command line gave; a breach of a schema is `schema <keyword>`, a file that is not YAML `not YAML`. The last line is the summary, with `module.FormatVersion`: `<module>: the module breaks no rule of the format <version>` (code 0) or `<module>: the format <version> refuses the module: N breaches` (code 1). An error goes to stderr as one line, `module-kit <command>: …`. There is no `--json`: a flag is an extension that breaks nothing;
  - **`build`** — the folder does not exist or is empty, or else code 2 and nothing is touched. A refusal by the rules: the report, as with `check`, code 1 and nothing on disk. The package is written to the folder `package` inside a temporary `.<name>.<random>` beside the target folder and moved to its place, and the temporary folder is removed whatever the outcome. Files are created with the permissions 0644 and folders with 0755, as the process's umask adjusts them. The temporary folder itself, which `os.MkdirTemp` gives to its owner only, the command does not turn into a shared one (SonarCloud `go:S2612`). The summary is a line with the package's ID, version and digest;
  - **the warnings** — first those of a number in a text of the whole module, `module.Report.Warnings`, as lines `<path> #/pointer: warning: <message>` in the order of places, then those of pictures, `picture.Warnings`, as lines `<path>: warning: <message>`; all after the breaches and before the summary, for a module that is rejected too. They do not change the exit code, and `build` does not print them;
  - **the tests** hold the commands to the vectors: each valid example of a whole module gives code 0, each vector of a rule with a module's folder code 1, with each line of a breach its rule and a place in the folder, and the summary counts only them; `build` of each vector of `packages/` gives the three texts byte for byte equal to the canonical texts of the vector, and `device/` equal to the module's files by the listing of the device part. A line of a warning is read apart, only by its place in the folder: a key of a vector of `task.no-leaks` in a text of the whole module is also a number in it. The tests copy the files of `spec` from `spec.Format` into a temporary folder: CI does not take the submodule. In `module`, `TestTemplatesOnEachCPU` holds the same report and numbers with `GOMAXPROCS` 1 and 4.
- **The command `index`** — `cmd/module-kit/index.go`. It is called by the recipe `release-files VERSION PREVIOUS` of `modules` inside the shared release:

  ```
  module-kit index (--previous <module-index.json> | --first) --tag vX.Y.Z
      (--withdrawals <withdrawals.yaml> | --no-withdrawals) --out <file> <package folder>...
  ```

  - **"None" — by a flag.** Exactly one flag of each pair; an empty `--previous`, a flag given twice or neither of a pair: usage and code 2. A missing file does not start a new index ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). The recipe needs only `if [ -z "$PREVIOUS" ]` and a check of whether `withdrawals.yaml` exists;
  - **the inputs** — the package folders that `build` wrote: each read by `modulepackage.Read` by the digest of the bytes of its `inventory.json`, with `device/`. The withdrawals: YAML by the format's rules of YAML, then `$defs/withdrawals`. The previous index: bytes, as the shared release gave it, and `moduleindex.Build` reads it;
  - **the output** — `--out`, which does not exist yet, through a temporary folder beside it, as `build` writes a package; to stdout, the tag and the number of packages;
  - **the codes** — 0: the index is written; 1: a refusal by the rules of the index, a line `<rule ID>: <module> <version>: <words>` for each and a line of the summary; 2: a previous index that cannot be read — one of a newer format version among them —, a tag, withdrawals not in their form, a folder that is not a package, input or output;
  - **the tests** — every vector of `indexes/` through the command, the packages by `build` of the vector's modules, the index byte for byte equal to the vector, and a refusal only by its rule.
- **The command `open`** — `cmd/module-kit/open.go`. The samples page build takes tasks and media files from a release of `modules` through the CLI of `module-kit`:

  ```
  module-kit open --index <module-index.json of the release> --latest <module-index.json of the newest release>
      --seed N [--seed N]... --out <folder> <package folder>...
  ```

  - **Inputs.** CI of `canvas` has already checked both indexes with the recipe `release-verify`, and has downloaded the packages by their entries. `--latest` is the same file when the release is the newest. The package folders are positional, as with `index` and `diff`: the command knows each by the digest of the bytes of its `inventory.json` and reads it by the chain, and of `device/` each file by the listing of the device part. An extra folder does no harm, and a release with nothing to open needs no folders. A package is read by the chain from the digest of the entry, and its inventory must name the entry's ID, version and format version, or else code 2. `--seed` is a seed of the format in decimal digits with no leading zero ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)): at least one, each once. The page build chooses the seeds.
  - **Which versions.** The index of the release names the versions, and the marks of withdrawal the command reads in the index of the newest release: only it knows of every withdrawal. `moduleindex.WithLatest` combines them, and a newest index that does not hold an entry of the release as it was is code 2. For each module, `moduleindex.Newest`: the newest by semver unwithdrawn version within the format's window, the rule of a new attempt in `api`. Each newer version that the command passed over is named on stdout — a withdrawn one with its reason, or one of another format version —, and a module without a version by a line `<ID>: no version to open`.
  - **`open-views.json`** — a canonical text, as a package's: `format: "module-kit-open-views"`, `version: 0`, `module_format_version` and `modules` in the order of IDs.
    - A module has `id`, `version`, `module_format_version`, `release` (the tag of the entry), `package_digest`, `manifest` — the open view of the manifest, from which the canvas takes `canvas.digit_height` —, and `tasks` in the order of IDs.
    - A task has `{"open_view_digest", "task"}`, and an instance of a template also `seed`, as a string, as a sample writes it. An instance for each seed, in the order of the numbers. It is the instance that an attempt with this seed would get: `template.AttemptInstance` among the `instances` of the package, then `template.Instance`; no solvers run.
    - `task` is the open view as a JSON value, before any type, `openview.TaskView`, and `open_view_digest` is the digest of its canonical text: whoever reads the text again gets the same digest.
    - The model of the form is `tutor-open-tasks` v0, but that file holds banks, not modules, and has no manifest, seeds or digests.
  - **The files of the device part** — `modules/<ID>/<version>/LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and `media/<path>`, byte for byte, only checked ones: the device part carries them to everyone who gets the content ([format text](../spec/format/README.md)). The layout is the same as that of a package's `device/`, and the path of a media file from the open view is found from `media/`.
  - **Codes** — 0 and 2: the command checks no format rules. The summary on stdout is a line per module with the number of open views, and a line with the folder.
  - **Tests** — every vector of `packages/`, the instances by the seeds of the vector `templates/missing-factor.yaml`, the media files, a withdrawn version and the codes 2.
- **The YAML reader** — `internal/yaml`, `Read(data []byte) (any, error)`, by the rules of YAML of the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **parsing** — `go.yaml.in/yaml/v4`, pinned in `go.mod`, into a tree of nodes. Around it are the passes of the package, each in its own file: the bytes and the level of the stream before parsing, and the replacement of U+0085, U+2028 and U+2029 by characters of the private use area (`text.go`), the errors of v4 by the rules (`errors.go`), the walk of the tree by the rules `yaml.*` (`walk.go`), and what YAML 1.2.2 rejects and v4 lets through (`layout.go`);
  - **the value** — `map[string]any`, `[]any`, `string`, `int64` for a number with neither a fraction nor an exponent, `float64` for the others, `bool`, `nil`. An integer that the reader gave as a double the canonical view writes as an integer;
  - **the refusal** — `*Refusal`: each breach is a rule ID or "not YAML", a line and a column in characters of the file as it is written, from 1, and a message. The module check puts them into its report, with the file's path;
  - **an anchor with a colon at the end of its name** — `&a:` and `*a:`: go-yaml cuts the trailing colons of a name off on purpose (yaml/go-yaml#109) and fails further along the line, with a reason that names no anchor. Then `colonAnchor` searches, before the place of the refusal, for `&` or `*` at the start of a node whose name by YAML 1.2.2 ends with a colon, and names `yaml.no-anchors` at it;
  - **the second implementation of `spec`**, `yaml.mjs`, reads by YAML 1.2.2 as this reader does, CR as a line break, `...` at the start of a stream and U+FEFF included;
  - **the tests** — the files of the format and the YAML test suite in `testdata/yaml-test-suite/` (`TestYAMLTestSuite`), whose every case passes: a case that `suiteUnpassed` names and that passes fails the test.
- **The module check** — the package `module`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **API** — `Check(ctx context.Context, fsys fs.FS) (Report, error)` checks the module whose folder is the root of `fsys`, with the solvers of templates one per CPU and with `ctx`, and `CheckFile(part string, data []byte) (Report, error)` checks a file of a part by itself, as an example of a part and a vector of a rule in a file. `CheckModule(ctx, fsys)` is the same `Check`, and in addition the numbers of the instances of each template of an accepted module, by task ID; the package build takes them. An error comes only when the folder or a file cannot be read or `ctx` is done, code 2 of the CLI. `Report` is the breaches `Breach{Rule, Path, Line, Column, Pointer, Message}` in the order of places: the path from the module's folder, `.` being the folder itself, the line and the column in the file, and the value as a JSON Pointer in URI fragment form, `#/id`. Every rule goes into the same report;
  - **the window of versions** — `FormatVersion`, the constant of `module/check.go` that the tests hold to `format/VERSION` of `spec.Format`, `ReadsFormat`, and `NewerFormat`: whether a format version is newer than `FormatVersion`, by the values of the numbers; by it the index build does not read a previous index of a newer version. The manifest names the version before anything else, and a module of a version outside the window gets one breach, `manifest.format-version`: the reader does not know its layout and rules. The same holds for `CheckFile`. A version that is not a string of the form MAJOR.MINOR is a breach of the manifest's schema by the reader's version. Without a manifest, and with a manifest that the YAML rules rejected, the module is checked by the reader's version;
  - **the layout** — the names as the folder's listing gives them, letter case and all; a name beginning with `.` is not the module's; a link and anything that is neither a file nor a folder is `module.layout`; `eval/` is in the layout, and the package does not take it. A file of a part is read by the YAML reader and checked against the schema of its part, and `module.file-ids` checks `id` against the name only in a file that both accepted;
  - **a breach of a schema** — a `Breach` with `Keyword`, the keyword of JSON Schema, and the place of the value in `Pointer`, without a rule, a line or a column. A file that the schema rejected breaks it and nothing else: the rules of its part do not read it, and the rules between parts run only when all the files of parts have passed the schema, as in `format-check` of `spec`. For the rules of parts, the check holds the values of the files that passed, by their paths, and a flag "all passed";
  - **files without a schema** — `module.media`: the path `media_path` and the signature of PNG, JPEG, WebP or MP3 by the first 14 bytes, four bytes for the header of an MP3 frame, as with WHATWG; `module.license` and `module.plain-text`: a breach for each fault of plain text, at its first place;
  - **the code against the format text** — the table of the layout, the rules `module.*`, the forms `local_id` and `media_path` from `common.schema.json` and `FormatVersion` the tests check against `spec.Format`, and `TestChecksTheFormat` checks that the modules and files of parts of `spec.Format` give no breaches but of the rule of their case, and an invalid example only breaches of the schema.
- **Schemas** — `internal/schema`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **the validator** — `github.com/santhosh-tekuri/jsonschema/v6`, pinned in `go.mod` (sources 4.5), by the schemas of `internal/schema/schemas/`: this is a copy of `schemas/` of `spec.Format`, embedded by `//go:embed`, and `TestSchemasAreSpecs` holds it byte for byte and by `$id`. Each schema goes into the validator by its `$id`, and the validator does not go to the network;
  - **API** — `Validate(location string, value any) []Error`: a schema by its file name, or a subschema by a pointer in the fragment, `task.schema.json#/$defs/check`, and each keyword that failed is an `Error{Keyword, Location, Message}`, and `Pointer` writes the place. Compilation is once, calls are from several goroutines. An unknown place, a schema that does not compile and a value not from JSON are a panic: that is a defect of `module-kit`;
  - **which keywords are named** — as in an example of the format: `$ref`, `allOf`, `properties`, `items` and `if`/`then`/`else` never, `anyOf`, `oneOf`, `not`, `contains` and `propertyNames` without what failed inside them, `unevaluatedProperties` at the object. The fields that a subschema that failed declares stay "unevaluated" beside it, as by 2020-12 and in ajv; the CLI writes such a breach as `schema <keyword>`;
  - **the workaround of a bug of the validator** — it does not copy the place of an error of `propertyNames`, and a neighbour overwrites it (santhosh-tekuri/jsonschema#276; sources 4.5). A vocabulary of its own, `propertyNames`, checks the names once more and gives an error at the right place, and the collection passes over the validator's error;
  - **annotations** the validator does not collect: the open view walks the schemas by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md) itself, as `format-check` does, and asks the validator only whether a subschema passes (the open view, below);
  - **the files of vectors** the vector test reads with `readVector`: the YAML rules, the vector's schema, then strictly a type of `formatv0`.
- **The open view** — by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **the walk** — `schema.Open(location, value)` in `internal/schema`, as `openViewOf` of `format-check` of `spec`. At a place there apply its subschema, what its `$ref` names, each branch of `allOf`, the branches of `anyOf` and `oneOf` that pass, `then` or `else` by `if`, and the branches of `dependentSchemas` whose key the object has. A field is open when at least one of its applied subschemas carries the mark `x-open` — under `properties`, a matching key of `patternProperties`, or `additionalProperties` —, an element of an open array is open, and the root is always open. Whether a branch passes is asked of `Validate` of the same subschema by a pointer, and a subschema that the walk reached by two paths is taken once;
  - **API** — `openview.Manifest`, `SkillGraph`, `Traps`, `Techniques`, `SolvingSteps`, `Lesson` and `Task`: the part as a value of a type of `formatv0`, the view as a value of a type of `formatv0/open`. A part that the schema does not accept is an error with all the breaches: the format defines the view only of a valid part. A view that the type of `open` does not hold is a panic, a defect of `module-kit`;
  - **the instance** — the open view of an instance of a template is `openview.Task` of `template.Instance`, and the vectors of `templates/` hold that it is built for each attempt;
  - **the numbers for `solve`** — `template.Inputs`: the parameters and derived numbers whose placeholders stand in the strings of the open view of the template as it is written, as `solverInputsOf` of `spec`;
  - **the digest** — `openview.TaskDigest`: `sha256:` of the canonical text of a task's open view, for an instance from `template.Instance`, with the numbers substituted. The format has no vector for it: `TestTaskDigest` and `TestInstanceDigest` hold the digests that the second implementation of `spec` computed;
  - **the vectors** of `open-view/`: `part.yaml` by the schema and a type of `formatv0`, `open-view.yaml` strictly by a type of `open`. The view of the walk is compared with the JSON value of `open-view.yaml`, as in `format-check`, and the view of the part's function with the value of the type of `open`.
- **The rules of parts** — the package `module`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **within one part** — `manifest.bands`, the rules of the skill graph `skill-graph.acyclic`, `known-skills`, `retired-ids` and `own-anchors`, and also `task.skill-roles`, `task.zones`, `lesson.areas`, the rules that the slot check shows by itself — `task.choice-key`, `task.choice-options`, `task.fraction-key`, `task.trap-values` and `task.predicate-expression` —, and `task.no-leaks` on the texts of the task itself. They read only their own file and run on each file that passed the schema, in `Check` and in `CheckFile`, as an example of a part. The five rules of the slot check read the check by its own verdicts, `verdict.OfSlot` and `verdict.Same`, and the predicate by `verdict.CheckPredicate`; a check of `number` or `fraction` with a placeholder outside a template breaks `task.placeholders` instead of them. `task.no-leaks` searches for a leak with the package `leak`, below. A template they read by its instances, and the rules of templates and `task.instances` run within a part too — below, "Templates";
  - **between parts** — `manifest.dependencies`, `manifest.rung-templates`, `traps.known-skills`, the rules of a task `known-traps`, `known-techniques`, `known-skills`, `own-anchors`, `known-band`, `known-pictures`, `age-categories` and `assessability`, `no-leaks` on the texts of the catalogs and the rung templates, and the rules of a lesson `known-skills`, `known-tasks`, `task-skills`, `known-pictures`, `age-categories`, `order` and `no-leaks`. They run only when all the files of parts have passed the schema, and do not find a part that the module does not have: a trap of a task in a module without a catalog is unknown. The picture of a task and of a lesson is searched for in the listing of `media/`, which `CheckFile` does not have — so they too are between parts;
  - **how they read** — a value that the schema accepted is read strictly into the type of the part of `formatv0` once, `schema.Decode`, and a breach names the place of the value: `#/bands/1/id`, `#/skills/<ID>/prerequisites/<number>`, `#/items/<number>/answer_slots/<number>/zone`. A cycle of prerequisites and a cycle of the order of lessons is a breach at each skill and lesson on it, by strongly connected components; an item with a verdict is an item at least one answer slot of which is checked by `number`, `fraction`, `choice` or `predicate`; the skills of an item are its own, or else the task's;
  - **tiers** — `module.Tiers`: tier 1 for a skill with no prerequisites among the module's skills, the others one tier above the highest prerequisite, and an anchor gives no tier. A graph with an unknown prerequisite or a cycle has no tiers: an error. The package build takes them;
  - **the tests** — `TestChecksTheFormat` holds that an example and a vector break no rule but that of their case.
- **The verdict** — the package `verdict`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **API** — `OfItem(ctx, item, values)` and `OfSlot(ctx, slot, value)`: the verdict of an item and of an answer slot by the values as they are written, and `nil` is nothing read. An answer slot is an `AnswerSlot` — the binding, the check, and the options of an answer slot bound `choice` —, and `AnswerSlots(item)` gives the answer slots of an item in order. The predicate on an integer in notation is computed by the sandbox with the limits `sandbox.Predicate` and the caller's `context`: `True` is `correct`, `False` is `not_yet`, and an error, a result that is not a `bool`, the limit of steps, the clock and a panic are `unchecked`, without a reason. `SlotVerdict` is a `Verdict` and a `Reason`, types of its own with the words of the format, and a copy of the trap value that matched, with its ID and the text for the learner; `ItemVerdict` is the verdict of an item and of its answer slots. `Same(slot, a, b)` tells whether two values are equal as the check compares them: the rules of `module` and the join of two readings take it. For the join, `verdict` also gives `NormalForm(slot, value)` — the value as the kind normalizes it, without the key and without Starlark, and whether it is in notation —, `ItemOf(slots)` — the verdict of an item from the verdicts of its answer slots, and without answer slots `unchecked` —, and the reasons of reading `Illegible`, `Ambiguous`, `NoFinalAnswer` and `SeveralFinalAnswers`. The package does not import protobuf;
  - **errors** give no verdict: a cancelled `context`, an item without answer slots, a value not in UTF-8, a check that the format does not accept — with a placeholder of a template, this is `ErrPlaceholder` —, a rubric — `ErrNoVerdict`; a `context` cancelled while the predicate runs gives its error. A check that breaks a format rule that it shows by itself does give a verdict, and a predicate that breaks `task.predicate-expression` gives `unchecked`. The rule itself is `CheckPredicate(expression)`: only `verdict` knows the name `value`;
  - **normalization** — steps 1–7 and the form of a choice, in the package `internal/normal`, which the leak check takes too. At the edge cases, as in `verdicts.mjs` of `spec`: the neighbours of a vulgar fraction are characters of the original string, steps 3–4 come after NFKC, there is no trimming between steps 5 and 6, so `7 rungs .` is not in notation, and the length of a unit is in characters. The key is read as written, and the values after normalization. A number is a `big.Rat`, and irreducibility goes by the numerator and the denominator as written;
  - **Unicode** — NFKC is `golang.org/x/text/unicode/norm`, pinned in `go.mod`, and White_Space, the category N and the simple lowercase mapping are the package `unicode` of the Go toolchain: both tables must be of one version of Unicode. The release names it by the constant `verdict.UnicodeVersion` — this is `normal.UnicodeVersion`, `norm.Version` —, and on the second line of `--version`; `TestUnicodeVersion` holds it, `norm.Version` and `unicode.Version` equal to the version that `verdict/verdict_test.go` names, which a raise of Unicode changes. If Go raises `unicode` before `norm` takes the same version, the test turns red on the toolchain of `module-kit`, but not at a consumer with another toolchain;
  - **where it diverges from the second implementation of `spec`** — `norm.NFKC` goes together with the Stream-Safe Text Process of Unicode (sources 5.7): after 30 non-starters in a row it inserts U+034F, and ICU in `verdicts.mjs` does not. There is no vector with such a run.
- **The observation** — the package `observation`, by the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md):
  - **API** — four functions, each with a rule of the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md) and a field of the vector: `Validate(items, o)` — `valid`, `Join(items, first, second)` — `joined`, `Verdicts(ctx, items, o, tapped)` — `verdicts`, `Essay(items, o)` — `submission`. The task is its items, by the types of `formatv0`; the observation is `*observationv1.Observation`; the taps are `Tapped{Place, Value}`, only those chosen, in the order of the task, as `api.v1.TappedChoice`; the verdict is `[]verdict.ItemVerdict`; the outcome of an essay is `Submission`: `SubmissionAssessment`, `SubmissionUnreadable` or `SubmissionUnchecked`;
  - **how they are called** — `api`: `Join`, then `Verdicts` on the joined observation, or on `nil` when there is none — a failure of AI, a limit, an invalid observation —; it keeps the joined observation and the taps, and repeating a verdict is the same `Verdicts`. In the reading mode `single` there is one reader's observation and no `Join`: `Verdicts` on it, which validates it, and on `nil` when it gives `ErrNotValid` or AI gave none; `api` keeps the observation only when it is valid, as it keeps the joined one. The eval runner: the truth of the markup and the reading of one reader — `Validate` and `Verdicts`, a pair — `Join` and `Verdicts`; an essay — `Essay`;
  - **validity** — the conditions of the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md), and beyond them a value not in UTF-8, which protobuf-go does not write. A reason goes by its own table `Unreadable` → `verdict.Reason`: a reason that the table does not know is not valid, and `TestReasonsAreThePackages` holds the table equal to the enumeration of the pinned tag. The error `ErrNotValid` names each condition broken;
  - **the checks of the answer slots** — before the reading: `Join` and `Verdicts` call `verdict.OfSlot` without a value on each answer slot that AI reads, and a check that the verdict rejects is an error, whatever was read;
  - **the join** — without `context` and without Starlark: an empty normal form is `UNREADABLE_BLANK` before the pairing; two values join when both are not in notation with one `NormalForm`, or both are in notation, equal by `Same` and with one verdict, reason and trap, and for a predicate one integer; when they join, the value of the first and the `crop_box` of the first or the second; two same reasons give that reason; everything else gives `UNREADABLE_AMBIGUOUS`. The joined observation is a new message. An essay is not joined: it has one reader;
  - **`unchecked`** — an observation that is not valid `Validate`, `Join` and `Essay` name by the error `ErrNotValid`, so an `unchecked` in `Verdicts` on a valid observation is the `unchecked` of a predicate. Which reason the eval report writes beside it is `spec`'s;
  - **protobuf** — `google.golang.org/protobuf` comes only with this package;
  - **the vectors** of `observation/v1/`, with the runner `observations`: the file strictly as JSON, the canvas and the items by the schema of a task and the types of `formatv0`, the observations by `protojson`, the joined one by `proto.Equal`; `TestObservationsRefuse` holds that a corruption of each field of a vector fails it.
- **The key of a task from paper** — the package `paper`, by the [contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md):
  - **API** — `Solve(formula)` — `*paperv1.Number` or a `Reason`; `Breach(answer)` — a `Rule` or none; `Key(solved, ofAI)` — `*paperv1.TaskKey`, one task's, with no statement, the solver's first, and with no answer and no provenance when AI's breaks a rule or is none; `Items(answer)` — one item, one answer slot `anywhere`, its check `number` or `choice` with `notation: options`, and no item for an answer of no kind, a task with no key, which the caller gives "unchecked"; `Brought(answer, o)` — a copy of the observation, each value by `BroughtValue`;
  - **how they are called** — `api`, at the first `SubmitWork` of an attempt from paper: `Solve` on the formula of the statement, and when it is not taken `Key` on the AI's solution; it keeps the key on the attempt, and on each Check `Brought` on each reader's observation, then `Join` and `Verdicts` of `observation` with `Items` of the key;
  - **the solver** — a parser of its own by the grammar, `paper/parse.go`, and an evaluation over linear parts a·x + b of `*big.Rat`, each part bounded to 100 digits, the two sides of an equation brought together too; the order of its reasons is the contracts text's;
  - **the vectors** of `paper/v1/` — the runners `solverVectors`, `answerVectors` and `verdictVectors`, `internal/vectors/paper_test.go`, each file strictly, a case by its own kind's fields and a verdict's `value` required, null too, its messages by `protojson`, and the key of a verdict's case one that `Breach` takes; fuzz tests `FuzzSolve` and `FuzzBroughtValue`.
- **The leak check** — the package `leak`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **API** — `For(task)` builds the `Search` of a task: the key of each check of `number`, `fraction` and `choice` of each item, except a key that the same search finds in a text that the learner sees with its item — the paragraphs and the `alt` of the statement, the `text` of the item, the `text` pieces of its line, the options of answer slots bound `choice` —, and each leak phrase. `Search.In(text)` is what of them was found, `[]Leak{Kind, Written, Item, Slot, Phrase}`, in the order of the task. A template is `ErrTemplate`: `api` builds the search on the instance of the attempt. A key of a number or a fraction not in the notation of its kind is not searched for, as in `leaks.mjs`. One `Search` searches from several goroutines;
  - **the search** — in the normal form: step 1, NFKC, steps 3 and 4 and the lowercase mapping, from `internal/normal`. A phrase is found as a substring at any place where an edge that is a character of a word has no character of a word outside it; a number is the longest run of the form of a number from a digit 0–9, with no character of a word outside it, with its value as a `big.Rat`, and a key is compared by magnitude. A byte not in UTF-8 is U+FFFD;
  - **the rules** — `task.no-leaks` and `lesson.no-leaks` in `module/leaks.go` (above, "The rules of parts"): a breach is per text, in the file of the task or the lesson, at the place of the text, and for a text of another part at the place where the task names it, and the message names the file and the place of the text. A template is searched through its instances, below, "Templates".
- **Templates** — the package `template`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **API** — `Instances(ctx, task)`: the breaches of the rules that a template breaks by itself — `task.template-names`, `task.template-expressions`, `task.placeholders`, `task.template-instances` and `task.template-solver`, `Breach{Rule, Pointer, Message}` —, or the numbers of each instance in the order of the counter, with the solver's results, `[]formatv0.Numbers`; an error only for a `context` that is done; `Inputs(task)` — the names of the numbers that `solve` gets; `Instance(task, numbers)` — the instance with these numbers, in a single pass, without expressions and Starlark; `Shown(template, numbers)` — the numbers of the parameters, as a breach names them; `AttemptInstance` — the number of the instance of an attempt, by the generator above;
  - **the order of the rules** — as in `format-check`: the names, the expressions, the placeholders and the solver's program, and the combinations are counted only when these are not broken; then an empty domain, more than 10,000 combinations, the first combination on which an expression fails or a derived number goes beyond ±(2^53 − 1) — and the count stops —, and not a single instance; then a solver that fails on an instance or before the first;
  - **expressions** — a parsing of its own of the subset, with the types `int` and `bool`, as `expressions.mjs` of `spec`, and on top of it `sandbox.CheckExpr`. An expression begins with a token and is nested no deeper than 64 levels, `maxDepth`, as `format-check` checks too, below the depth go.starlark.net parses, so the dialect parses every expression of the subset. They are evaluated by `sandbox.Compile`, once per expression, and by `Func.Call` on each combination, without a limit of steps and without a clock;
  - **placeholders** — the strings of a task outside `template`, from its JSON value by `schema.ValueOf`, are read from left to right: `{{` and `}}` are a brace, `{name}` is a placeholder. A breach — one per string, at its place; a result of the solver stands only in the key and the trap value of `number` and `fraction`, in the solution and in a leak phrase;
  - **the solver** (`template/solver.go`) — the program is checked by `sandbox.CheckProgram` together with the names, the expressions and the placeholders, and after the count it runs once — `sandbox.Exec` with the limits `sandbox.Solver` —, and `solve`, a function of the program with one positional parameter — with no keyword-only parameters, `*args` or `**kwargs` —, is called on each instance in order on the same thread, with a frozen dict of the numbers of `Inputs`, inserted in the order of the names. Its result is a dict of exactly the results, each an `int` within ±(2^53 − 1), and the results go into the numbers of the instance. A failure is one breach at `#/template/solver/program`, with the numbers of the instance or "before the first instance", and the count stops: "on left = 4, to_ben = 6, solve fails: fail: 0 whole numbers fit the story, at 11:13 in solve". The count looks at the caller's `context` before each combination, and the solver runs with it; its cancellation is an error, not a breach;
  - **in the module check** (`module/templates.go`) — a template is expanded once per path: within a part, the rules of a template, `task.instances` — each keyword of the schema of a task at each place that an instance does not pass, by its first instance, without the cascade of `unevaluatedProperties` —, and on each instance that the schema accepts, the rules of the slot check and `task.no-leaks` on the texts of the task; between parts, `task.no-leaks` on the texts of other parts and `lesson.no-leaks` by the keys of each instance. A breach of a rule of a task at a place is that of the first instance that gives it there, with its numbers: "on the instance first = 2, second = 7:"; an instance that breaks the same place otherwise is seen once the place is fixed. The rules that do not read the strings of a task — skills, the band, zones, pictures, categories, the rules of a lesson except the leak rule — read the template as it is written, once. The instances of a template with a solver the rules read with the results, as the instances of a template without one. The templates of a module `module.Check` expands one per CPU, with the caller's `context`, and `CheckFile` one at a time, with `context.Background()`;
  - **the vectors** of `templates/`, with the runner `templates`: the template by `module.CheckFile` of its JSON, the number of instances, for a solver `inputs`, and for each seed the number, the numbers with the results of its own run of the solver, the instance as a JSON value and its open view.
- **The module index** — the package `moduleindex`, by the [format text](../spec/format/README.md):
  - **the build** — `Build(Release)`: `Release{Previous, First, Tag, Packages, Withdrawals}` — the previous index as a file, or an explicit "no previous one", `First`, the packages `*modulepackage.Package`, the tag `vX.Y.Z` and the withdrawals by the type `formatv0.Withdrawals`, `nil` being no withdrawals. The result is `module-index.json`, the canonical text of an index of the version `module.FormatVersion`; the CLI writes it to disk. The entries of the previous index stand as they were, a package of a version that the previous index names keeps that version's entry, the others get a new one — the format version of the inventory, the package digest, the tag and `teaches` by the skills of the graph —, and the withdrawals are marked last. The packages go in the order of ID, version and digest, so the result does not depend on the order of the input;
  - **the previous index** the build reads as the build writes it: by the schema of its own version, as a canonical text and with the `teaches` of each entry in order — and first its format version, at the place that every version keeps: an index of a newer version, `module.NewerFormat`, means that the CLI in CI of `modules` was rolled back. Each entry stands "as it was" in an index that the build's schema accepts. An index that cannot be read — not JSON, not by the schema, not a canonical text, `teaches` out of order, a member or a reason of a later version, a newer format version — is `ErrPrevious`, and the release fails rather than start a new index;
  - **the refusal** — `*Refusal{Breaches}`, `Breach{Rule, Module, Version, Message}`, in the order of module, version and rule, one for each breach of `module-index.entries-kept`, `one-package`, `known-withdrawals` and `version-above`. A new version below a published one goes into the index all the same, so that its withdrawal gives no second breach, as in `index.mjs` of `spec`. An error of the call — a tag that is not a tag, withdrawals not by `$defs/withdrawals`, a package not of the form that `modulepackage.Build` and `Read` give, a first release without packages — is `ErrRelease`, and comes before any refusal: the rules are read only in a release where everything is in form;
  - **reading** — `Read(data)`: the members of the root and of an entry at the places that every version keeps, and a member at another place is not read; the form of each place is by the schema of the index, except `withdrawn`: a version is withdrawn whatever its reason, and `Withdrawn` carries the reason as written. An entry of a format version outside the window `Read` gives together with the others — version comparison needs every published version —, and `Newest` passes over it. `Read` does not require the canonical text: it is part of the format's version. The error is `ErrNotIndex`;
  - **the version of a new attempt** — `Newest(index, id)`: the newest by semver version of a module that is not withdrawn and is within the format's window, `module.ReadsFormat`. `api` takes it, and the command `open` takes it on the index of the release with the marks of the newest index that `WithLatest` combined (above). Versions are compared by `internal/semver`: numbers without a bound are held as text and compared by length, then by digits;
  - **the vectors** — the valid examples of an index the runner `examples/valid` also holds to what the schema cannot say, as `indexFormProblems` of `spec` does: the root names the version of `format/VERSION`, and `teaches` is in order (`TestIndexForm`). The vectors of `indexes/`, with the runner `indexes`: the package of each module of `modules/` by `modulepackage.Build`, the previous index by its canonical text, the index built byte for byte equal to the canonical text of the vector's `index`, a refusal only by the rule `refusal`, and `Read` reads both indexes back into the values of the vector.
